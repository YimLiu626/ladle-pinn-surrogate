Introduction

Ladle furnace (LF) refining is a critical secondary metallurgy process for controlling steel cleanliness, thermal uniformity, and compositional homogeneity . During bottom argon stirring, the injected gas forms a buoyancy-driven plume that induces strong recirculation in the molten steel bath . This plume-driven motion enhances mixing and promotes the upward transport of inclusions toward the slag layer . Excessively strong stirring may intensify slag–metal interfacial deformation, expose the steel to the atmosphere through slag eye formation, and increase re-oxidation risk .

CFD has become a standard tool for studying ladle hydrodynamics . The discrete phase model (DPM) coupled with VOF has emerged as a practical approach for simulating argon-stirred ladle flow . However, high-fidelity three-dimensional multiphase simulations remain expensive—a single case may require upwards of 36 hours.

Machine learning surrogates can provide predictions orders of magnitude faster than numerical simulation . Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) incorporate governing-equation residuals into training , and have been applied to various fluid mechanics problems . Challenges include training instability and sharp interfaces across large density ratios .

A key challenge specific to ladle-flow surrogates is the multiphase nature—three immiscible phases spanning four orders of magnitude in density. We propose a VOF-masked PINN framework: in pure-steel cells ($\alpha_{\rm steel} \to 1$), the VOF mixture equations reduce to single-phase incompressible RANS. Restricting physics to the steel phase avoids ill-conditioned multiphase residuals. A binary injection-position parameter m ∈ {0, 1} enables a single model for both center-bottom and eccentric-bottom blowing.

Contributions: (1) VOF-masked PINN with soft phase restriction, (2) m parameterization for cross-position generalization, (3) six-baseline comparison, (4) uncertainty quantification via multi-seed ensemble, (5) surrogate achieving R² = 0.983 with 5-second inference.

VOF-Informed Domain Decomposition

In pure-steel cells ($\alpha_{\rm steel} \to 1$), the VOF mixture equations reduce to single-phase incompressible RANS. Soft pointwise weights restrict physics constraints: $w_{\rm data} = \text{clamp}(\alpha_{\rm steel}, 0.01, 1.0)$ and $w_{\rm pde} = w_{\rm data} \cdot 0.5[1 + \cos(\pi \cdot \min((z_{\rm top}-z)/T, 1))]$ with $z_{\rm top} = 1.85$ m, T = 0.20 m. Verified phase stratification confirms identical layering for all Q values (Table 4, Fig. 2).

Network Architecture and Training

The model maps (x, y, z, Q, m) to (û, v̂, ŵ, p̂) via an 8 × 384 SiLU MLP (1.04M parameters). A 6 × 128 variant is used for ablation. Inputs are mapped to [−1, 1]; outputs use z-score normalization per channel. The continuity residual $\mathcal{R}_{\rm cont} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}$ is evaluated in physical space via automatic differentiation with proper scaling. Wall no-slip is enforced on steel-region boundary points (∼42k points). Training uses Adam (lr  = 10⁻³), batch size 8192, 2500 epochs, on an RTX 5070 GPU (8 GB, ∼5 min). Inference: ∼5 s for 9.3 × 10⁵ cells.

Experimental Setup

Training uses center-blowing Q = 40, 60, 80 NL/min; Q = 100, 120 NL/min are extrapolation tests; three eccentric cases (Q = 40, 80, 120 NL/min, m = 1) are cross-position tests. Six baselines: MLP 6 × 128, MLP 8 × 384, DeepONet (p = 50), Kriging GPR (2000 inducing pts), POD-ROM, KAN. Metrics: R² on velocity magnitude and per-field R². Results

Loss Weight and Physics Ablation

A $\lambda_{\rm cont} \times \lambda_{\rm bc}$ sweep (6 × 128) found $\lambda_{\rm bc} = 0$ optimal; continuity weighting is robust over four orders of magnitude (Fig. 3). Three ν_(t) treatments (mixing-length, constant, none) yield identical R² (0.825). Continuity alone provides the only measurable gain: +0.014 R² (Table 6). The momentum residual—incomplete without DPM buoyancy—does not enhance performance.

Baseline Comparison

Physics term ablation (Fig. 4a) and baseline comparison (Fig. 4b) are shown together. Continuity alone provides the only measurable gain (+0.014 R²); the momentum residual and boundary-condition terms do not enhance performance. The six-baseline comparison shows MLP 8 × 384 (1.04M params) at R² = 0.983. DeepONet fails (R² = −0.72)—branch–trunk factorization poorly captures the global plume structure. Kriging diverges on the 9.3 × 10⁵-cell 4D input space. POD-ROM requires structured-grid interpolation; KAN encountered toolchain barriers on Windows.

[]

Cross-Position Generalization

A center-only model (m = 0) collapses on eccentric data (R² = −1.12 to −0.70). Joint training with m ∈ {0, 1} recovers R² = 0.94–0.97 for eccentric cases while maintaining R² = 0.974 for center-blowing (Fig. 5). Multi-seed stability: 0.972 ± 0.003 (center), 0.945 ± 0.007 (eccentric). For eccentric Q = 120 NL/min, the plume peak shifts downward (z ≈ 0.70 m vs. ∼1.80 m for Q = 40, 80) due to slag eye venting—confirmed by two independent CFD runs.

Pointwise Accuracy and Convergence

Centerline velocity profiles (Fig. 6) show close agreement between CFD and PINN across all eight configurations. The 8 × 384 model achieves $R^{2}_{\rm int}=0.980$, $R^{2}_{\rm ext}=0.986$. All five Q values exceed R² = 0.975 (Fig. 7). Error distribution: σ = 0.003 m/s, with errors concentrated in the high-gradient plume core. Convergence is smooth, reaching R² > 0.90 by epoch 1000 (Fig. 8).

Discussion

Pointwise prediction errors are concentrated in the high-gradient plume core (Fig. 12), with the histogram showing comparable error distributions for center and eccentric Q = 120. The VOF-based domain decomposition succeeds because in pure-steel cells, the mixture equations reduce to single-phase RANS exactly. Momentum residuals do not help because DPM bubble buoyancy—the actual plume driver—is absent from the homogeneous RANS formulation. The m parameterization proves both necessary (center-only model fails on eccentric data) and effective (joint model recovers). For the 8 × 384 model, continuity adds negligible gain (R² = 0.980 vs. 0.980 pure data), suggesting that at sufficient capacity, physics regularization becomes unnecessary. Limitations: (1) steady-state only—transient slag eye dynamics require time-resolved training, (2) no explicit bubble physics, (3) single ladle geometry, (4) three training operating points. Future work: transient slag eye, inclusion removal prediction, multi-ladle generalization.

Conclusion

An 8 × 384 SiLU MLP with VOF-informed domain decomposition achieves R² = 0.983 for center-blowing with near-zero extrapolation degradation. Joint m ∈ {0, 1} training recovers R² = 0.94–0.97 for eccentric-blowing. VOF filtering is the dominant design factor (+0.344 over hard thresholding). Six baselines were compared; DeepONet, Kriging, POD-ROM, and KAN encountered scalability or implementation barriers. The surrogate predicts the full field in ∼5 seconds. Future work addresses transient slag eye dynamics and inclusion removal prediction.
